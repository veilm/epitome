On September 25, 2023, OpenAI announced that it was beginning a gradual rollout of voice conversations and image input for ChatGPT. The company said the features would reach ChatGPT Plus and Enterprise users over the following two weeks: voice on the iOS and Android apps as an opt-in setting, and image input on all platforms. OpenAI said it intended to extend access to other groups, including developers, later.

The voice feature lets users hold spoken, back-and-forth conversations with ChatGPT. Users could enable it through the mobile app's New Features settings, select one of five voices, and start it through a headphone control. OpenAI said the system combined a new text-to-speech model, which can produce speech from text and a short voice sample, with its open-source Whisper speech-recognition system. The available voices were created in collaboration with professional voice actors.

Image input allows users to submit one or more photos, screenshots, or documents and discuss their contents with ChatGPT; the mobile apps also provide a drawing tool to identify an area of interest. OpenAI said the capability was powered by multimodal GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 models. Examples offered in the announcement included identifying a landmark, planning a meal from refrigerator and pantry photos, troubleshooting equipment, interpreting a graph, and helping with a photographed math problem.

OpenAI framed the limited release as part of its gradual-deployment safety strategy. It noted that realistic synthetic speech could enable impersonation or fraud, and said it was restricting the underlying voice technology to voice chat and related collaborations, citing Spotify's Voice Translation pilot as an example. For image input, OpenAI reported red-team and alpha testing in areas including extremism and scientific proficiency. It said the models can hallucinate about people and are unsuitable for unverified high-stakes use; it had technically limited ChatGPT's ability to analyze or make direct statements about people to address accuracy and privacy concerns. The article also cautioned that the model was better at transcribing English text than many other languages, particularly non-Roman scripts, and advised non-English users not to rely on it for transcription.

OpenAI said its work with Be My Eyes, an app for blind and low-vision people, informed the feature's design and limitations. The announcement credited Alec Radford, Tao Xu, and Jong Wook Kim for core voice research, and Raul Puri, Jamie Kiros, Hyeonwoo Noh, Long Ouyang, and Sandhini Agarwal for core vision-deployment research.
