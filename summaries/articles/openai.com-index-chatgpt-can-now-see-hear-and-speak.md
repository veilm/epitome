On September 25, 2023, OpenAI announced that it would begin rolling out voice conversations and image input for ChatGPT. The initial release was limited to ChatGPT Plus and Enterprise users over the following two weeks: voice was to be available as an opt-in feature in the iOS and Android apps, while image input was to be available on all platforms. OpenAI said it intended to extend access to additional users and developers later.

The voice feature let users speak with ChatGPT and receive spoken responses, with five selectable voices. OpenAI said it combined a new text-to-speech model, which could produce human-like audio from text and a short sample of speech, with Whisper for speech-to-text transcription. The company stated that the available voices were created in collaboration with professional voice actors. It described possible uses including hands-free questions, storytelling, and resolving conversations or debates.

For image input, users could submit one or more photos, screenshots, or documents and ask questions about them; mobile users could also draw on an image to direct attention to a particular area. OpenAI said this capability was powered by multimodal GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, and suggested uses such as troubleshooting an appliance, planning meals from pantry or refrigerator contents, interpreting graphs, and receiving hints on photographed math problems.

The announcement emphasized gradual deployment and safety limitations. OpenAI said realistic synthetic voices could enable impersonation or fraud, so it was initially restricting the technology to voice chat using voices developed with directly collaborating actors. It cited Spotify's contemporaneous Voice Translation pilot as another controlled use of the technology. For image input, OpenAI reported testing with red teamers and alpha testers, including in extremism and scientific-proficiency contexts. It said it had limited ChatGPT's ability to analyze or make direct statements about people because of accuracy and privacy concerns.

OpenAI also cautioned that vision models could hallucinate, and that users should verify outputs in high-risk or specialized settings. Although it described the model as proficient at transcribing English text, it said performance was poor for some other languages, particularly those using non-Roman scripts, and advised non-English users not to rely on it for transcription. The article linked this deployment approach to OpenAI's work with Be My Eyes and its image-input system card.
